A discovery session, replayed

The R·E·D Method

The recorded working session in which the method was built — a marketing consultant applying his own discovery process to a business owner's practice.

R Reveal Your Offer · E Engage and Convert · D Develop Your Audience

Frame: this is the origin session. The consultant uses the discovery approach — later named RED — to organize a business owner's scattered expertise. The method is being assembled as the session runs.

Opening

Business owner

I've built websites and campaigns for years — the technical work isn't the problem. What I can't do is turn what I know into a program a company can buy. My IP is scattered across projects and notes.

Consultant

Then that's the engagement: organize what you know into a framework — easy to understand, easy to consume, easy to sell, easy to scale. I'll run my discovery process on your practice, and write the process itself down as we go.

Two-sided engagement: the business owner gets his IP organized; the consultant documents the process — which becomes the method. Discovery-by-doing.

Opening · The agreement

Business owner

Writing it down as in… building the method during the session?

Consultant

Yes. The process is the product. By the end you'll have your program, and I'll have the framework that produced it. Three areas, in order: who you serve, how interest becomes commitment, how people find you consistently.

The agenda lands here: offer, conversion, audience — sequence matters.
R

Reveal Your Offer

Define the practice: who it serves, what it delivers, how it is described, and how it is structured. The consultant names this stage "Reveal Your Offer" as it opens — first entry in the framework.

Stage R: ideal client, offer currency, marketing message, curriculum. The stage names are being coined live — that's part of the story.

Stage R · Reveal Your Offer

Consultant

First question: who does your work help best? Not the widest audience — the ones who get the strongest result.

Business owner

Officially? Any company with a website. Which is exactly the problem — it's everyone, so it's no one. I've been picked by whoever showed up, not chosen by anyone in particular.

Consultant

Then we define the profile: role, frustrations, what they've already tried. Write down module one — identifying your ideal client. Specific beats broad.

Module 1 coined. The business owner's real habit — serving whoever shows up — is the generic-audience problem the module solves.

Stage R · Reveal Your Offer

Consultant

Next: what does a company actually walk away with? Not deliverables — the result. Time back, clearer positioning, revenue they can point to. That's the offer's currency.

Business owner

For mine? Clarity and structure — the feeling of finally having an organized program instead of a folder of files. Which is funny, because that's what I sell other people too.

Consultant

Say that again — that's the insight. Module two: clarifying the offer's currency. Offers are bought for results, never process. Marketing names the currency.

Module 2 coined. The business owner discovers his own currency while stating it — the method applies to itself.

Stage R · Reveal Your Offer

Consultant

First, the message: one sentence a past client could repeat about what changed for them. Site, emails, calls — all reuse it. Module three: creating unique marketing messages.

Business owner

One sentence, everywhere. Mine is close to: "We turn what your company already knows into programs that sell."

Module 3 coined. The owner's one-sentence message is literally the framework being built — self-application again.

Stage R · Reveal Your Offer

Consultant

Last one: structure your sessions and materials into a curriculum a prospect can review before starting — module four: organizing the curriculum. Understandable, consumable, easy to say yes to.

Business owner

That's the piece I've never had. Everything I know, in an order someone else could walk.

Module 4 coined. The offer is now packaged — ready for the conversion stage.

Stage R · Written summary — added to the framework

ProfileOne profile: role, frustrations, prior attempts.
CurrencyThe offer stated as the result delivered — time and pricing confidence, in this example.
MessageOne sentence, reused across site, email, and calls.
StructureThe program organized as a curriculum a prospect can review in advance.
Pause here. Stage R output feeds every later stage; nothing downstream works without it.
E

Engage and Convert

Build the path from first contact to enrollment: capture, follow-up, and the enrollment conversation. Second stage of the framework, coined as the session reaches it.

Stage E: lead magnet, funnel, prescriptive sales process.

Stage E · Engage and Convert

Consultant

Someone discovers your business. What do they receive first?

Business owner

An empty contact form, honestly.

Consultant

We'll build a lead magnet: one useful resource solving a small, real problem, exchanged for an email — module one: developing an effective lead magnet. It demonstrates the work instead of describing it.

Module 1 coined. The magnet demonstrates the work.

Stage E · Engage and Convert

Consultant

Then the funnel around it: landing page, thank-you page, scheduled follow-up — module two: building the sales funnel. Every step leads somewhere specific.

Business owner

So interest gets held instead of leaking out the sides.

Module 2 coined. Capture (magnet) then guidance (funnel); no dead ends.

Stage E · Engage and Convert

Business owner

The part I avoid is the enrollment conversation. I either talk too long or never ask for the decision.

Consultant

Then we take improvisation out of it — a prescriptive process: diagnose first, where the prospect is and what they've tried; then prescribe, whether the program fits, plainly. Module three: mastering a prescriptive sales process.

Business owner

Steps I can follow on a call, and the fit decides — not my stamina. That's sellable as a method, not just advice.

Module 3 coined. Pressure-free selling is a byproduct of structure; the owner immediately sees it as productizable IP.

Stage E · Engage and Convert

Consultant

One note as you write this into your own program: a clear no is a fine outcome. It's what makes the yeses reliable — for you, and for the companies you'll run this process with.

Close stage E on the ethics: the no is part of the method.

Stage E · Written summary — added to the framework

CaptureA lead magnet that solves one small, real problem.
PathLanding page → thank-you page → scheduled follow-up. No dead ends.
EnrollmentA prescriptive call: diagnose, prescribe, invite. Fit decides.
Stage E output: interest now has a defined route to enrollment.
D

Develop Your Audience

Make discovery consistent: content, follow-up on interested visitors, and paid advertising. The third stage — the session now has a full shape to name.

Stage D: content strategy, retargeting/remarketing, paid advertising.

Stage D · Develop Your Audience

Consultant

The conversion path is in place. Where do new companies find you?

Business owner

Referrals when I'm lucky. I post without a pattern; emails go out on guilt.

Consultant

Content gets a schedule and a source, drawn from the Stage R message — module one: content strategy. Nothing invented fresh each week.

Business owner

One message, many placements. That I can maintain.

Module 1 coined. Content repurposes Stage R.

Stage D · Develop Your Audience

Consultant

And visitors who don't opt in on the first visit? They get retargeted with the same message — module two: retargeting and remarketing. Recaptured at lower cost than a first impression.

Business owner

A net behind the first visit. Every visitor gets two chances instead of one.

Module 2 coined. Remarketing recaptures visitors the funnel already attracted.

Stage D · Develop Your Audience

Consultant

Last one: paid advertising. Once the funnel converts at a known rate, ads are a scale mechanism — module three: effective use of paid advertising. We scale what's measured.

Business owner

Define, convert, then amplify. Three letters' worth of process that I'd been doing in fragments for years.

Consultant

Which is why we ran the session in this order. The framework names itself: Reveal · Engage · Develop. R.E.D.

Module 3 coined, and the session closes the loop: the framework is named from the process that built it. The acronym arrives here, earned.

Stage D · Written summary — added to the framework

ContentScheduled posts and emails, all drawn from the Stage R message.
RetargetingInterested visitors re-shown the same message at lower cost.
AdvertisingPaid traffic scaled against a measured client value.
Stage D output: discovery is now systematic. R, E, D together form the full engagement model.
