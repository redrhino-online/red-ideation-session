A marketing framework, presented as a conversation

The R·E·D Method

How coaches, consultants, and solopreneurs make it easy to understand, easy to buy, and easy to scale what they sell.

What follows is a consultation, replayed: a consultant and a coaching client, working the method end to end. Both roles are played by the same practitioner — the consultant, following his own medicine.

Frame it: this is a model enrollment conversation. The method has three acts — Reveal, Engage, Develop. Watch for what the consultant asks, not just what he says.

Act 0 · The first call

Client

I run a coaching practice. I'm good at the work — my clients get results. But the business side feels like guesswork. I post when I remember, I email when I have something to say, and every so often someone books a call out of nowhere.

The consultant writes three words on a pad.

Consultant

You don't have one problem. You have three wearing a trench coat: you can't see who you're for, you don't have a path that turns interest into commitment, and you have no engine that brings people back tomorrow. Guesswork isn't a character flaw — it's what's left when those three are invisible.

Client

So where do we start?

Consultant

With the only question that matters. Not "how do I get more traffic?" — but: what exactly are you selling, and to whom? Act one.

Method note: diagnose before prescribing. The three struggles map to the three stages — audience clarity, conversion, traffic. Never sell a solution to an unnamed problem.
R

Reveal Your Offer

Act I — clarity and precision about what you sell and who it's for.

Stage R: four modules — ideal client, offer's currency, marketing messages, coaching curriculum.

Act I · Reveal Your Offer

Consultant

Who benefits most from your work?

Client

Anyone who wants to grow their business, really. I don't want to exclude anyone.

The consultant nods slowly, the way you do before bad news.

Consultant

"Anyone" is why your message lands with no one. Pick the person who gets the most from you — their frustrations, their ambitions, what they've already tried. That's not a limitation. That's a magnet.

Consultant

Second question: what's the currency you're actually selling? Not "sessions" — what do clients walk away holding? Reclaimed hours? Confidence? Revenue they can point at?

Client

…Time, mostly. And the confidence to charge what they're worth. Huh. I've never written that down.

Method note: modules 1–2. The ideal client is a magnet, not a restriction. The "currency" exercise converts features into the value clients actually buy.

Act I · Reveal Your Offer

Consultant

Last test: if I called three of your past clients and asked what changed for them, would I hear the same sentence three times?

Client

Honestly? I'd probably hear three different answers.

Consultant

Then we write the sentence for them — one message, in their words, that your website and your emails can repeat until it's true. And while we're at it: walk me through your program, end to end. We'll organize it into a curriculum a stranger could follow — so it's easy to understand, easy to consume, and easy to say yes to.

Method note: modules 3–4. Unique messaging + organized curriculum = the offer is now commercializable IP: easy to understand, easy to sell, easy to scale.

Act I · The transformation

Before

A scattergun approach. Generic messages that resonate with no one in particular. Clients who are a stretch to serve. Marketing that feels like shouting into a crowded room — and the quiet suspicion of being left behind.

After

A laser picture of the ideal client. A value proposition in one breath. Messages that make the right people feel seen. A program a stranger could follow — and want to join.
Stage R closes when the offer is easy to understand and easy to say yes to. Everything downstream inherits this clarity.
E

Engage and Convert

Act II — a path that turns interest into commitment, without pressure.

Stage E: three modules — lead magnet, sales funnel, prescriptive sales process.

Act II · Engage and Convert

Consultant

Someone just discovered you. What do they get?

Client

They… could email me?

Consultant

We'll build them something better — a lead magnet: one genuinely useful tool that solves a small real problem, traded for an email. It demonstrates your expertise instead of claiming it.

Client

And then they just… sit on my list?

Consultant

Then we give them a path — a funnel: a landing page that makes one promise, a thank-you page that starts the relationship, and follow-up that earns the call. No dead ends.

Method note: modules 1–2. The magnet attracts; the funnel guides. Every page has exactly one job.

Act II · Engage and Convert

Client

Can I say the uncomfortable part? I hate selling. I either freeze or talk for forty minutes and never ask for the business.

Consultant

Then we'll take the improvisation out of it. A prescriptive process: diagnose first, prescribe second — like any good clinician. You're not persuading anyone; you're determining whether you can actually help. Sometimes the honest answer is "I'm not your person" — and that answer is what makes every other yes trustworthy.

Client

So selling stops being a performance… and becomes an assessment.

Method note: module 3. Pressure-free selling is a byproduct of structure, not personality. Diagnosis → prescription → invitation.

Act II · The transformation

Before

Interest arrives and evaporates. A funnel that's more of a concept than a system. Sales conversations avoided, over-talked, or improvised — and decent traffic converting at a fraction of what it could.

After

A magnet worth exchanging an email for. A funnel with no dead ends. Enrollment calls that follow a process both people can trust — and a conversion rate that reflects the work you actually do.
Stage E closes when interest has a reliable path to commitment. The system converts; the person just follows it.
D

Develop Your Audience

Act III — an engine that brings the right people back, tomorrow and the day after.

Stage D: three modules — content strategy, retargeting/remarketing, paid advertising.

Act III · Develop Your Audience

Consultant

The funnel works now. But funnels are plumbing — where does the water come from?

Client

That's the part I never figure out. I post into the void.

Consultant

Then we build a content strategy — social and email working from the same messages we wrote in act one, so you're never inventing something new every morning. And for everyone who visited and left? Retargeting — a quiet reminder of why they came, bringing them back for a fraction of what first contact costs.

Client

So the audience becomes an asset instead of an audience.

Method note: modules 1–2. Content repurposes the act-one message; remarketing recaptures the visitors the funnel already earned.

Act III · Develop Your Audience

Consultant

Last piece: paid ads. When the funnel converts reliably, ads aren't a gamble — they're a throttle. You already know what a client is worth; you're just renting attention at a price that leaves margin. We scale what's proven instead of hoping what's new.

Client

First the system, then the spending. In that order.

Consultant

In that order. That's the whole method.

Method note: module 3. Ads amplify a proven funnel; they never rescue an unproven one.

Act III · The transformation

Before

Posting into the void. Emails that go out on guilt, not rhythm. Visitors who never return. Growth that depends on being accidentally discovered — and ad spend that feels like a slot machine.

After

Content with a job on every channel. Visitors who get chased gently and come back. An audience that compounds — and paid traffic applied like a multiplier on a machine that already works.
Stage D closes when traffic is a system, not a hope. R + E + D together form the whole engine.

The consultation ends

Simple. Powerful.
Focused on results.

Close on the sequence, not the slogans. R before E before D; the order is the method.
